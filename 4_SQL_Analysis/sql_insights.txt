# SQL Analysis – Loan Approval Prediction Analysis

## 1. Overview

SQL analysis was performed on the cleaned loan dataset to understand the factors associated with loan approval and rejection.

**Dataset Summary:**

* Total Applications: 614
* Approved Loans: 422
* Rejected Loans: 192
* Overall Approval Rate: 68.73%

---

## 2. Overall Loan Approval

The overall loan approval rate is **68.73%**, with 422 approved applications out of 614 total applications.

### Key Insight

The majority of applications were approved, but 192 applications were rejected, showing that there are still significant differences between applicant profiles.

---

## 3. Credit History Analysis

Applicants with a good credit history (`Credit_History = 1`) had an approval rate of **79.05%**.

### Key Insight

Credit History is one of the strongest factors associated with loan approval in this dataset.

---

## 4. Education Analysis

| Education    | Approval Rate |
| ------------ | ------------: |
| Graduate     |        70.83% |
| Not Graduate |        61.19% |

### Key Insight

Graduate applicants had a higher approval rate than Not Graduate applicants.

---

## 5. Gender Analysis

| Gender | Approval Rate |
| ------ | ------------: |
| Female |        66.96% |
| Male   |        69.12% |

### Key Insight

The difference between male and female approval rates is relatively small. Therefore, Gender does not appear to be a major differentiating factor in this dataset.

---

## 6. Marital Status Analysis

| Marital Status | Approval Rate |
| -------------- | ------------: |
| No             |        62.91% |
| Yes            |        71.82% |

### Key Insight

Married applicants had a higher approval rate than unmarried applicants.

## 8. Self-Employment Analysis

## 9. Property Area Analysis

## 10. Applicant Income Analysis

# Final SQL Business Insights

Based on the SQL analysis, the major findings are:

1. **Overall loan approval rate is 68.73%.**
2. **Credit History is the strongest visible factor associated with loan approval.**
3. Applicants with `Credit_History = 1` had a **79.05% approval rate**.
4. **Credit History = 1 + Semiurban Property Area** had the highest combined approval rate at **86.21%**.
5. Graduate applicants had a higher approval rate (**70.83%**) than Not Graduate applicants (**61.19%**).
6. Semiurban property applicants had the highest property-area approval rate (**76.82%**).
7. Married applicants had a higher approval rate (**71.82%**) than unmarried applicants (**62.91%**).
8. Self-Employment showed almost no difference in approval rates.
9. Applicant Income and Loan Amount averages were very similar between approved and rejected applications.
10. High loan requests had a lower approval rate (**63.53%**) than Low and Medium loan categories.
11. Some loan-term categories had very small sample sizes, so their percentages should be interpreted cautiously.
12. The analysis shows **associations and patterns**, not proof that any single factor directly causes loan approval or rejection.

---

# Conclusion

The SQL analysis provides a deeper understanding of loan approval patterns in the dataset.

The strongest observed pattern is the relationship between **Credit History and Loan Approval**. Applicants with good credit history consistently showed much higher approval rates across different education and property-area groups.

These SQL findings will be used as a foundation for the **Power BI dashboard**, where KPIs, charts, filters, and interactive visualizations will be created to communicate the analysis clearly.
